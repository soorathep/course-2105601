Eigenvalue problems, the SVD and conditioning: derivations

Six derivations for Session 2. About two hours; do them before the midterm.

How to use this set

Work each problem on paper before you look at the final answer. The final answer lets you check your result; it does not show the derivation, which is the point of the exercise. These problems are in the style of the midterm.

Problem 1 · A non-symmetric matrix

Diagonalize M = [[4, 1], [2, 3]]: find its eigenvalues and eigenvectors, and write Mk = VΛkV⁻¹. Are the eigenvectors orthogonal? What does Mkx approach for large k?

Final answer. λ = 5 with v = (1, 1) and λ = 2 with v = (1, −2); not orthogonal (M is not symmetric). Mkx ≈ 5kc1(1, 1) for large k, with c1 = (2x1 + x2)/3.

Problem 2 · Rayleigh bounds

For M = [[2, −1, 0], [−1, 2, −1], [0, −1, 2]], use the trial vector x = (1, 1, 1) to bound the smallest eigenvalue, then find the exact eigenvalues and compare.

Final answer. xᵀMx/xᵀx = 2/3, so λmin ≤ 0.667; exactly λ = 2 − √2 = 0.586, 2, 2 + √2 = 3.414.

Problem 3 · An SVD by hand

Find the singular values of A = [[3, 0], [4, 5]] from AᵀA, the condition number, and the error of the best rank-1 approximation in the 2-norm.

Final answer. AᵀA = [[25, 20], [20, 25]] has eigenvalues 45 and 5, so σ = √45 = 6.708 and √5 = 2.236; κ = 3; the rank-1 error is σ2 = 2.236 (Eckart–Young).

Problem 4 · Normal equations square the condition number

Using the SVD X = UΣVᵀ of a full-column-rank X, show that κ(XᵀX) = κ(X)². What does this mean for solving least squares with 8-digit data when κ(X) = 10⁵?

Final answer. XᵀX = VΣ²Vᵀ, so its singular values are σi²; κ(XᵀX) = 10¹⁰ loses about 10 digits, more than the data hold, while QR or SVD on X loses about 5.

Problem 5 · Correlation of an intercept and a slope

For y = β0 + β1u fitted to n points with independent errors, show that corr(β̂0, β̂1) = −ū/√(mean of u²). Evaluate it for u = 1, 2, 3 and for u = 101, 102, 103, and explain the difference.

Final answer. From (XᵀX)⁻¹: −0.926 for u = 1, 2, 3 and −0.99997 for u = 101, 102, 103; the intercept is defined at u = 0, far outside the second data set.

Problem 6 · Identifiability of a decay

For c = c0e−kt sampled at t1, t2, write the 2 × 2 sensitivity matrix S with respect to (c0, k) and show that it becomes singular when t1 = t2. What does that mean for an experiment?

Final answer. S = [[e−kt₁, −c0t1e−kt₁], [e−kt₂, −c0t2e−kt₂]], det S = c0e−k(t₁+t₂)(t1 − t2); repeated samples at one time cannot separate c0 from k.
