Boundary value problems and Sturm–Liouville theory: derivations

Six derivations for Session 7. About two hours; do them before the midterm, which covers Sessions 1 to 7.

How to use this set

Work each problem on paper before you look at the final answer. The final answer lets you check your result; it does not show the derivation, which is the point of the exercise. These problems are in the style of the midterm.

Problem 1 · A pellet with a film resistance

Derive the overall effectiveness factor ηo of a first-order slab with a Robin condition at the surface, and show that the pellet and the film act as resistances in series. Evaluate ηo at φ = 2, Bi = 5, and say which resistance is larger.

Final answer. ηo = (tanh φ/φ)·Bi/(Bi + φ tanh φ); at φ = 2, Bi = 5: η = 0.4820 and ηo = 0.3479. 1/(ηφ²) = 0.519 against 1/Bi = 0.2, so the pellet resistance is larger.

Problem 2 · Sturm–Liouville form and a weight function

Put u″ + 2u′ + λu = 0, u(0) = u(1) = 0 in Sturm–Liouville form. Find the eigenvalues and eigenfunctions, and state the weight with which the eigenfunctions are orthogonal.

Final answer. Multiply by e2x: (e2xu′)′ + λe2xu = 0, so p = w = e2x; λn = 1 + n²π², un = e−x sin nπx, orthogonal with weight e2x.

Problem 3 · The Fredholm alternative

For −u″ − π²u = x − a, u(0) = u(1) = 0, find the value of a for which a solution exists, and explain why the solution is then not unique.

Final answer. a = 1/2, from ∫01(x − a) sin πx dx = (1 − 2a)/π = 0; any multiple of sin πx can be added to a solution.

Problem 4 · A Green’s function with a symmetry condition

Find the Green’s function of −u″ = f on (0, 1) with u′(0) = 0 and u(1) = 0, and use it to solve the uniform case f = 1.

Final answer. G(x, s) = 1 − max(x, s); for f = 1, u = (1 − x²)/2.

Problem 5 · The spherical pellet

Solve r−2(r²u′)′ = φ²u, u′(0) = 0, u(1) = 1 with the substitution u = v/r, and derive η = 3u′(1)/φ² for the sphere. Give its small- and large-φ limits.

Final answer. u = sinh(φr)/(r sinh φ), η = (3/φ²)(φ coth φ − 1); η ≈ 1 − φ²/15 for small φ and η ≈ 3/φ for large φ.

Problem 6 · Small dispersion

Expand the Wehner–Wilhelm exit concentration for large Pe and show that u(1) ≈ exp(−Da + Da²/Pe). Check it at Da = 2, Pe = 100 against the exact conversion 0.8594.

Final answer. u(1) ≈ exp(−Da + Da²/Pe); at Da = 2, Pe = 100 this gives a conversion of 0.8591, against 0.8594 exactly.
