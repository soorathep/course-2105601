Scaling and perturbation methods: derivations

Five derivations for Session 6. About two hours; do them before the midterm.

How to use this set

Work each problem on paper before you look at the final answer. The final answer lets you check your result; it does not show the derivation, which is the point of the exercise. These problems are in the style of the midterm.

Problem 1 · A regular expansion to second order

Find the root of x³ − x + ε = 0 near x = 1 as x ~ 1 + εx1 + ε²x2. Check your result numerically at ε = 0.05 and state the order of the error.

Final answer. x ≈ 1 − ε/2 − 3ε²/8; at ε = 0.05 the root is 0.97399 and the three-term result is off by about 7 × 10⁻⁵ (order ε³).

Problem 2 · A boundary layer: where, how thick, and the composite

For εy″ + 2y′ + y = 0, y(0) = 0, y(1) = 1, with 0 < ε ≪ 1: find the outer solution, locate the layer and its thickness, find the inner solution, match, and write the uniformly valid composite.

Final answer. Outer yo = e(1−x)/2; layer at x = 0 with thickness ε/2; inner A(1 − e−2x/ε) with A = e1/2; composite yc = e(1−x)/2 − e1/2 − 2x/ε.

Problem 3 · The small parameter of the QSSA

Starting from the mass-action equations for S + E ⇌ C → E + P, choose scales for S, C and t, and show that the scaled complex balance has the form ε dc/dτ = s − (s + κ)c/(1 + κ). Give ε, and show that ε ≤ E0/(S0 + KM).

Final answer. ε = k2E0/(k1(S0 + KM)²) = [E0/(S0 + KM)]·[k2/(k1(S0 + KM))], and the second factor is at most k2/(k−1 + k2) ≤ 1.

Problem 4 · Large Thiele modulus for any order

For a slab with u″ = φ²un, derive the large-φ effectiveness factor with the first-integral argument. Then treat a zero-order reaction (n = 0): find the depth at which the reactant is used up and the effectiveness factor when that depth is less than the half thickness.

Final answer. η ≈ (1/φ)√(2/(n + 1)). For n = 0, u = (φ²/2)(z − z0)² in the active zone z0 < z < 1, with 1 − z0 = √2/φ; the active depth is √2/φ of the half thickness and η = √2/φ (when φ > √2).

Problem 5 · A secular term

For y″ + y + εy³ = 0, y(0) = 1, y′(0) = 0, find y1 in the naive expansion y = y0 + εy1. Identify the secular term and estimate when the expansion fails. (cos³t = (3 cos t + cos 3t)/4.)

Final answer. y1 = −(3/8) t sin t + (cos 3t − cos t)/32; the secular term −(3/8)t sin t makes εy1 comparable with y0 when t ~ 1/ε.
